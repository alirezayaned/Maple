Why we measure AI quality

Every time this tool runs, the AI writes a morning briefing summarizing your alert data. Before that briefing reaches you, we automatically check it against the same data the scoring engine uses. The scorecard on the main report tells you how trustworthy this particular briefing is, so you know when to rely on it and when to look at the tables instead.

The AI doesn't search for information (this isn't a search engine). It reads the structured data our scoring engine produces and writes a narrative summary of it. The metrics below measure how well that summary represents the data it was given.

G

Groundedness

Is every claim traceable to the data?
Weight: 35%

What it checks

Two things: entity grounding (every vehicle, driver, work order, and fault code the AI mentions actually exists in the data) and relation grounding (associations like "V-137 at North Yard" or "V-108 is a dispatch blocker" match reality).

Why it's the highest weight (35%)

A briefing that mentions trucks that don't exist, or says a truck is grounded when it's not, causes real operational damage. This is the most dangerous way an AI can fail: making up facts that look authoritative. We give this the highest weight because a single fabricated vehicle or false blocker status can derail the entire morning dispatch.

High score: "V-137 (North Yard) has an oil pressure fault after WO-5540 repair." — Every entity exists, the depot is correct, the work order matches.
Low score: "V-270 needs immediate attention" (V-270 doesn't exist), or "V-145 must not dispatch" (V-145 is not actually a blocker).
C

Coverage

Did the AI mention everything important?
Weight: 25%

What it checks

The AI should mention every dispatch blocker, every post-repair fault, and key data quality facts (duplicates removed, vendor severity discrepancies). We count how many of these critical items from the scoring engine's output appear in the briefing.

Why it matters

A briefing that mentions V-137 but forgets V-108 could lead you to dispatch a truck with two critical engine faults. The whole point of the briefing is to surface everything that needs attention — a gap in coverage is a gap in your decision-making.

High score: All 4 blockers mentioned, both post-repair faults called out, duplicate count noted, vendor overcount flagged.
Low score: Mentions 2 of 4 blockers and skips the Hamilton backfill vehicles entirely.
N

Correctness

Are the numbers and facts right?
Weight: 25%

What it checks

When the AI says "2,380 raw alerts" or "134 duplicates removed" or "42 HIGH-priority," we compare those numbers to the actual counts from the scoring engine. We also catch false claims like "no duplicates found" when there were 134, or "30% duplicates" when the real rate is about 6%.

Why it matters

Numbers build trust. If the AI says "30% duplicates" when the actual rate is 6%, it creates a false impression that the data quality is terrible — which could undermine confidence in the whole system. Getting the numbers right is table stakes for credibility.

High score: "134 duplicates removed from 2,380 alerts, 42 ranked HIGH" — matches exactly.
Low score: "30% duplicates" (actual ~6%), or "5,000 raw alerts" (actual 2,380).
A

Actionability

Does it help you make tonight's decisions?
Weight: 15%

What it checks

Three things: (1) dispatch blockers should appear before lower-priority items, because you need to know what can't go out before planning what can; (2) post-repair faults should be called out, because those are the most surprising and dangerous findings; (3) the briefing should include next-step language ("inspect," "do not dispatch," "open a work order") — not just describe the situation.

Why it matters

A briefing can be perfectly accurate but still useless if it buries the "V-137 can't dispatch" note at the bottom and leads with idling statistics. The order and framing of information shapes decisions. This metric checks whether the AI organised its output to actually help you act, not just to report.

High score: Leads with blockers, calls out post-repair faults, tells you to "re-inspect V-137 before dispatch" and "open a work order for V-108."
Low score: Leads with "327 idling alerts" and only mentions blockers in passing at the end. No next steps suggested.

The bottom line

The AI briefing is a convenience — a 60-second summary so you don't have to read 2,000+ alert rows. But every number in the stats bar, every vehicle in the blockers list, and every row in the action items comes from the deterministic scoring engine, not the AI. That engine is tested with 79 automated checks and produces the same output every time for the same data.

When the AI scores an A, the briefing is a trustworthy narrative. When it scores lower, you know exactly which dimension failed and can look at the tables instead. Either way, the data is always there.
