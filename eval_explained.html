What each metric means and why it matters for your dispatch decisions

Why we measure AI quality

Every time this tool runs, the AI writes a morning briefing summarizing your alert data. Before that briefing reaches you, we automatically check it against the same data the scoring engine uses — the "ground truth." The scorecard on the main report tells you how trustworthy this particular briefing is, so you know when to rely on it and when to look at the tables instead.

F

Faithfulness

Are the AI's claims actually true?
Weight: 30%

What it checks

When the AI says "V-137 is at North Yard" or "V-108 had a post-repair fault after WO-5512," we verify those claims against the actual data. Does V-137 really belong to North Yard? Was WO-5512 really the work order for V-108? Every factual claim the AI makes is cross-referenced.

Why it matters

If the AI puts V-137 at the wrong depot, the Hamilton maintenance lead wastes time looking for a truck that's actually at North Yard. Wrong facts lead to wrong decisions.

100% score: "V-137 (North Yard) has an oil pressure fault after WO-5540 repair." — Every fact matches the data.
Low score: "V-137 (Airport Hub) needs attention." — Wrong depot.
H

Hallucination

Did the AI make things up?
Weight: 25%

What it checks

We verify that every vehicle ID (V-101 to V-160), driver ID (D-01 to D-60), work order number, and fault code the AI mentions actually exists in the data. We also check that the AI doesn't call a vehicle a "dispatch blocker" when it isn't one — that's a hallucinated status, not just a hallucinated name.

Why it matters

This is the most dangerous failure. If the AI says "V-270 needs immediate attention" and V-270 doesn't exist, someone wastes time looking for a ghost truck. Worse, if it says "V-145 must not dispatch" when V-145 is actually fine, you lose a truck from your fleet for no reason. We weight this 25% of the total score.

100% score: All mentioned vehicles, drivers, WOs, and DTCs exist in the data. No false blockers identified.
Low score: AI mentions "V-270" (doesn't exist) or "D-53" (out of range) or says V-145 is a blocker (it isn't).
C

Completeness

Did the AI mention everything important?
Weight: 20%

What it checks

The AI should mention every dispatch blocker, every post-repair fault, and key data quality facts (like duplicates removed and vendor severity discrepancies). We count how many of these critical items appear in the briefing.

Why it matters

A briefing that mentions V-137 but forgets V-108 could lead you to dispatch a truck with two critical engine faults. The whole point of the briefing is to flag everything that needs attention — missing an item defeats the purpose.

100% score: All 4 blockers mentioned, both post-repair faults called out, duplicate count noted, vendor overcount flagged.
67% score: Mentions 3 of 4 blockers and skips V-108. The ops manager might dispatch V-108 without knowing about the overheating.
N

Numerical Accuracy

Are the numbers right?
Weight: 15%

What it checks

When the AI says "2,380 raw alerts" or "134 duplicates removed" or "42 HIGH-priority," we compare those numbers to the actual counts from the scoring engine. We allow a 15% tolerance (rounding and paraphrasing can shift numbers slightly), but anything beyond that is flagged.

Why it matters

If the AI says "30% duplicates" when the actual rate is 6%, it creates a false impression that the data quality is much worse than it is. Numbers build trust — wrong numbers destroy it.

100% score: "134 duplicates removed from 2,380 alerts" — matches exactly.
Low score: "30% duplicates" when actual is ~6%, or "5,000 raw alerts" when actual is 2,380.
R

Relevance

Does the AI focus on what matters most?
Weight: 10%

What it checks

Dispatch blockers should appear before lower-priority action items. Post-repair faults (the most surprising and dangerous findings) should be emphasized. If the AI discusses driver behaviour, it should include the firmware context that explains why some drivers' numbers look worse than they are.

Why it matters

If the briefing leads with an idling alert and buries the "V-137 can't dispatch" note at the bottom, the ops manager might skim the top and miss the critical action. The order of information shapes decisions.

High score: Leads with blockers, then post-repair faults, then action items.
Low score: Leads with "327 idling alerts" before mentioning any blocker.

Overall Grade Scale

GradeScoreWhat it means
A90%+ Briefing is reliable. Safe to read and act on as-is.
B80-89% Good overall, but double-check any items the scorecard flagged.
C65-79% Some gaps or inaccuracies. Use the tables below the briefing as your primary source.
D50-64% Significant problems. Rely on the tables, not the briefing text.
FBelow 50% Briefing is unreliable. It was likely replaced with the template fallback.

The bottom line

The AI briefing is a convenience — a 60-second summary so you don't have to read 2,000+ alert rows. But every number in the stats bar, every vehicle in the blockers list, and every row in the action items comes from the deterministic scoring engine, not the AI. That engine is tested with 79 automated checks and produces the same output every time for the same data.

When the AI scores an A, the briefing is a trustworthy narrative. When it scores lower, you know exactly which dimension failed and can look at the tables instead. Either way, the data is always there.
